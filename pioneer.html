The UNMUTED Origin Story

Long before the bright lights of the Yale University stage or the high-stakes arenas of Johannesburg, Adoyo Jashon Ondolo walked the corridors of East African schools when the air was heavy with a different kind of tension. In those days—before the abolition of corporal punishment—the school staffroom was often a place of shadowed silence, where the only sound louder than a heartbeat was the rhythmic thwack of a cane.

It was in these rooms that Jashon, a young educator with the discipline of a First Dan Black Belt and the soul of a poet, witnessed a recurring tragedy. He saw brilliant minds reduced to trembling statues. He watched students, caught in the crosshairs of a misunderstanding or a minor infraction, suffer physically and emotionally simply because they lacked the tools to articulate their truth. They weren't necessarily guilty; they were just muted. They had no shield of logic, no bridge of rhetoric, and no voice to stop the descending blow.

For Jashon, a man whose life has been a symphony of English literature and the rhythmic precision of music, this was an affront to the very essence of education. He realized that a student without a voice is a student without a future. This spark of indignation—this refusal to let the "powerless" remain silent—became the foundation for UNMUTED Debate.

He didn't just want to teach students how to argue; he wanted to teach them how to exist.

The Architect of Eloquence

Leveraging his decade-plus of experience as a Head of Academics and a leader at Mountain View School, he forged a platform where the "unmuting" of a child became a sacred mission. Under his mentorship, UNMUTED blossomed into more than a coaching clinic; it became an elite forge for global citizens.

The UNMUTED Legacy

Today, when Jashon stands before a room of Grade 9 students or prepares a delegation for a Global Round in Malaysia, he isn't just coaching a team; he is rectifying the ghosts of those old staffrooms. He has replaced the fear of the cane with the power of the "Point of Information."

In the world of UNMUTED, a student's voice is the ultimate equalizer. Jashon has ensured that whether his students are discussing the nuances of IGCSE literature or the complexities of global politics, they will never again be silenced by an authority they cannot respectfully challenge.

Adoyo Jashon Ondolo has taken the heavy silence of the past and orchestrated it into a roaring chorus of confident, articulate, and truly unmuted leaders. The staffrooms may have changed, but the mission remains: Give a child a voice, and you give them the world.

World Scholar's Cup — Official Adjudication Script

As delivered by UNMUTED's Head Adjudicator at Global Rounds

"Welcome to the Team Debate event of the World Scholar's Cup!

After I read the motion, you will have 15 minutes to prepare. You MAY NOT use ANY devices, notes, or outside materials. Please put them away now. [PAUSE]

Each of you may keep only one new sheet of notes taken during these 15 minutes. You will then have up to four minutes per speaker, with no interruptions allowed; I will knock once when you have one minute left and twice when you are out of time...

Now, to help me fill out this scoring form: Which of you is the Affirmative team? Show me your nametags, and please sit on my left. Which of you is the Negative team? Show me your nametags, and please sit on my right.

Today's motion is ______. You have 15 minutes to prepare, starting now.

[Time is up] First affirmative speaker, is your letter A, B, or C? Thank you. Please rise, join us at the front of the room, and begin when you feel ready.

[After speeches] Before I announce the winning side, each team must give up to 90 seconds of feedback to the other team. Your feedback must be kind, courteous, and constructive...

This round goes to the [AFFIRMATIVE/NEGATIVE] team! Now: please cross the bench and congratulate one another.

— Complete adjudication protocol as practiced by UNMUTED at Yale TOC & Bangkok Global Round
